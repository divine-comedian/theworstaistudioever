# Critique communication and usability

Review the visible page before reading the explanatory concept. A first-time visitor should be able to identify the audience, service, primary action and result from the surface itself. The creator's explanation cannot repair missing context.

Judge the landing page and demo for their different jobs. The landing page should explain a concrete proposition and lead naturally to the demo. The demo should organize information and controls around the source product's main workflow. Look for continuity of identity and promises without demanding identical layouts.

Assess specificity through visible evidence:
- Can you name the audience-derived visual cues beyond its name in a heading?
- Does the source service remain recognizable through its organization and actions?
- Do typography, imagery, spacing and color form a coherent system?
- Does the product preview explain an action, and does each supporting image add useful information?
- Does the actual copy communicate a comic setup and turn to an outsider?

Walk through the task as a newcomer, a returning user and someone using a narrow screen or keyboard. Use their goals to identify concrete obstacles. Do not invent demographic assumptions or fictional research findings. Prefer recognition over requiring visitors to remember a label, value or selection from another section.

Consider the most consequential friction first: an unclear proposition, competing primary actions, unreadable content, an unavailable control, misleading feedback, or a selection that cannot be completed. Distinguish these from optional polish. A preference for another typeface or palette is not a defect when the approved direction communicates well.

Use the existing review contract. Findings need an exact location, observed evidence, user impact and an observable acceptance condition. Preserve effective jokes and visual choices. Do not create a separate report, scoring system, persona exercise or review agent.
